# Draper Norwood — Brand Voice and Visual System

## Purpose

This file defines how Draper Norwood should sound, feel, look, and present itself.

Use this file when creating website copy, LinkedIn posts, visuals, case studies, design prompts, AI image prompts, Claude Code prompts, sales language, and brand assets.

## Relationship To Other Source Files

Use `01-master-context.md` as the governing source.

Use this file to protect:

- voice
- copy tone
- visual taste
- design restraint
- brand consistency
- public-facing expression

When in doubt, Draper Norwood should feel sharper, cleaner, calmer, and more commercially grounded.

## Brand Feel

Draper Norwood should feel:

- sharp
- restrained
- premium
- intelligent
- grounded
- strategic
- modern
- calm
- commercially serious
- high-trust
- execution-focused
- slightly philosophical, but not abstract
- precise, but not sterile

It should not feel:

- trendy
- loud
- hype-driven
- overly corporate
- generic agency
- startup cliché
- AI-generated
- mystical without business grounding
- over-designed
- desperate for attention

## Core Brand Idea

Draper Norwood gives stronger form to real value.

The work is about taking what is already true, useful, capable, or valuable and helping it become clearer, more credible, and more effective in the world.

## Voice Principles

### 1. Clear Before Clever

The writing should be intelligent, but not obscure.

Avoid clever lines that do not increase clarity.

### 2. Strategic, Not Decorative

Every line should serve positioning, trust, usefulness, or momentum.

### 3. Philosophical, But Grounded

Draper Norwood can use deeper ideas, but those ideas must connect to business reality.

Good:

“The market does not meet your work through your private understanding. It meets the form you give it.”

Bad:

“Your brand is the energetic portal through which your future self manifests abundance.”

### 4. Premium Through Restraint

Do not over-explain.
Do not over-sell.
Do not over-style.

Confidence is communicated through precision.

### 5. Practical Force

The language should carry weight, but remain usable.

Draper Norwood should sound like it can think and build.

## Preferred Copy Style

- strong declarative sentences
- restrained rhythm
- plain but elevated language
- clean structure
- specific nouns

## Visual System Principles

### 1. Restraint Over Noise

Avoid heavy gradients, chaotic backgrounds, excessive effects, or trendy visual clutter.

### 2. Typography Carries Authority

Typography should feel deliberate, clean, and premium.

Strong hierarchy matters more than decoration.

### 3. Negative Space Is A Feature

Draper Norwood should not feel crowded.

Space communicates confidence.

### 4. Proof Should Look Designed, Not Loud

Case studies, metrics, and proof assets should feel like internal intelligence reports, executive summaries, or polished studio documentation.

Avoid looking like social media screenshots dumped onto a page.

### 5. AI Should Be Invisible Unless Relevant

The company can use AI heavily, but the output should not look like generic AI.

AI is leverage behind the work, not the aesthetic.

## Copy Tone Examples

Good Draper Norwood tone:

“Your business may already have the proof. The issue is that the proof is not yet organized into a form the market can understand.”

“Draper Norwood helps close the gap between what a company actually delivers and how clearly the market can perceive it.”

“Not advice for its own sake. Work that becomes structure, assets, and measurable business movement.”

Avoid this tone:

“We help visionary founders unlock next-level growth through transformative digital solutions.”

“Your brand deserves to shine in a crowded marketplace.”

“We combine creativity and innovation to elevate your digital presence.”

## LinkedIn Voice

LinkedIn posts should feel human, intelligent, grounded, and specific.

They should not sound like:

- launch post templates
- agency announcements
- AI-written thought leadership
- fake vulnerability
- generic clarity content
- content marketing filler

Best LinkedIn territory:

- founder reflection
- strategic insight
- lessons from building
- the gap between value and perception
- why form matters
- public presence
- client work without overselling
- proof without bragging
- building Draper Norwood in public with restraint

## Brand Sentence

A useful internal sentence:

Draper Norwood exists to help real value take stronger public form.

## Final Standard

Every Draper Norwood asset should feel like it was made by someone with taste, judgment, strategic range, and execution ability.

Not loud.
Not generic.
Not overbuilt.

Clear, structured, crafted, and commercially useful.
